# PR187/208 performance — October 27–28, 2026

[Performance overview](README.md) · [Hardware and cache definitions](hardware.md)

**Finding:** Ada raw read/gather rates were stable in these comparisons, while
four-BD warm calibration showed a +10.17% B/A loop-time change. Perlmutter
measured about **2 seconds of extra first-batch time with sharing enabled
versus disabled in PR187**, but about **1.1 seconds versus the pre-PR187 base**.
At 10,000 events, sharing-on versus the base also showed mean increases of
**2.14 seconds in the remaining loop** and **3.34 seconds in total process
wall time**. These observations do not establish a startup-only cost. Setup,
first-use work, remaining processing and cleanup all matter; their
rank-maximized timings are not additive. Remaining-loop equivalence within
±5% is unresolved. Both PRs merged; this report closes the investigation with
those limits recorded.

## Ada: paired A/B/C on local storage

Job **42114353**, **sdfada016**, ran October 27 and completed 12 diagnostic
preflights and 96 clean timed samples. Each condition has four paired
repetitions with balanced XY/YX ordering. One Smd0 and one EB fed one or four BDs on GPU0 (L40S); raw used four
BDs. Input was run 387, `mfx101210926`, 32 Jungfrau panels, streams 005–009,
10,000 events. All revisions used batch size 20, stream depth 1, KvikIO eight
threads and 1-MiB tasks, with CPU fallback. Software was CuPy 14.0.1,
KvikIO 26.10, CUDA runtime 12.9 and Open MPI 5.0.8.

Raw used an explicit 6-GiB budget per BD. Calibration used automatic budgets,
960 MiB of requested constants, bulk reads on, and no full calibrated-image
D2H in clean timing. Calibration was measured warm only. The staged data
occupied 335,656,487,644 bytes; the timed payload was 335,571,760,000 bytes.
Warm measured prefixes were 100% resident before and after each sample;
cold maximum prefix residency was below 0.000014%.

## Perlmutter: sharing off/on and the first completed batch

Job **60415698**, **nid008517**, ran October 28 and completed 36 clean samples:
six balanced A/B-off/B-on triples at each of 1,000 and 10,000 events. The saved
scheduler record gives `StartTime=2026-10-28T00:38:30`; the October 27 date in
the artifact directory names is the campaign preparation date. Four BDs shared
GPU0 (A100-SXM4-80GB), with one Smd0 and one EB. All arms used an explicit 6-GiB/BD
budget, batch size 20, one stream, eight KvikIO threads, 1-MiB tasks and CPU
fallback. MPI was Cray MPICH 9.1.0.794; CuPy/KvikIO/CUDA runtime versions matched
the Ada campaign. Run 442, `mfx101232725`, used 32 panels, streams 004–008 and
768 MiB of requested constants. This is a different dataset and GPU from Ada.

B-off and B-on use the same B source with the existing
`PSANA_GPU_SHARE_CONSTANTS` switch set to `0` and `1`, respectively. B-off
retains peer discovery but uses private constants; no product patch is needed
to disable sharing. A owns private constants and ignores the new switch. The
comparison isolates the sharing choice more closely than A/B, which also
includes peer discovery and changed initialization.
Private Lustre input prefixes were client-cold before each sample. There was
no local SSD stage and no warm-Lustre claim; OSS/server caches were uncontrolled.

The first-batch boundary is delivery of each BD's first 20 events plus
completion of a CUDA event on the work stream. There is one boundary
synchronization per BD, with no extra synchronization on subsequent batches.
`remaining_s` is each BD's own `loop_s - first_batch_s`, then maximized across
BDs. The table gives means of per-sample maximum-BD timings. Setup is outside
the loop; manager close is measured within the loop. Independently maximized
columns must not be added together. Process wall includes launch/setup/teardown.

The approximately two-second first-batch penalty is clear at both lengths.
Remaining-loop means were +3.09% and +3.36%, but their intervals extend outside

events the whole-loop mean was +7.14%, with an interval spanning zero.

In absolute time, the mean extra remaining loop grew from 0.13 seconds at
1,000 events to 1.67 seconds at 10,000 events. The latter is comparable to the
1.97-second first-batch penalty. The mean extra whole loop grew from 2.15 to
3.75 seconds. These independently maximized durations are not additive.

These are Lustre-client-cold rates, not local-NVMe bandwidth measurements.

### Comparing PR187 with the pre-PR base

The sharing-toggle comparison above answers a different question from adopting
PR187 with its default sharing enabled. Against A, B-off's first batch was
0.97 seconds faster at 1,000 events and 0.81 seconds faster at 10,000 events.
B-on's first-batch increase relative to A was therefore **1.04 and 1.15
seconds**, respectively, rather than the approximately two-second B-on/B-off
difference.

At **10,000 events**, the mean timing changes relative to the pre-PR base were:

| Mean timing change | Sharing off − old base (B-off − A) | Sharing on − old base (B-on − A) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Setup | +1.20 s | +0.42 s |
| First batch | -0.81 s | +1.15 s |
| Remaining loop | +0.47 s | +2.14 s |
| Whole loop | -0.44 s | +3.31 s |
| Total process wall | +0.54 s | +3.34 s |

These are descriptive mean differences over the same six paired repetitions,
not proof that every difference is a persistent regression. The independently
maximized phase timings cannot be summed to reconstruct wall time. Manager
close is included in the loop; its mean change relative to A was +0.0013 s for
B-off and +0.2067 s for B-on.

B-off's faster first batch comes with longer setup outside the loop and a
slightly higher mean process wall time at 10,000 events. At 1,000 events,
B-off's setup was +0.79 s, first batch −0.97 s and process wall −0.52 s versus A.
Thus first-batch timing alone does not establish an overall private-path
speedup. Initialization work or waiting may have shifted between timing
regions, but the measurements do not establish that mechanism. The private
refresh timers differ by only about 0.22–0.24 s between A and B-off, insufficient
to explain the entire 0.81–0.97 s first-batch difference; inclusive timers are
not an additive decomposition either.

Interpret the results using three separate points:

1. **Sharing-toggle cost — B-on versus B-off:** enables sharing within the same
   PR187 build and fixed budget. It consistently adds about two seconds before
   the first completed batch, while remaining-loop effects are variable.
2. **Delivered revision cost — B-on versus A:** measures adopting PR187 with
   sharing enabled. This includes changes in setup, first-use work, remaining
   processing and cleanup. At 10,000 events, the mean total wall-time increase
   is 3.34 s, not the 1.15 s measured at the first-batch boundary. At 1,000
   events, the corresponding increases are 1.81 s and 1.04 s.
3. **Unresolved attribution:** the remaining-loop difference is substantial in
   seconds at 10,000 events and varies across repetitions. First-batch timing
   localizes part of the difference; it does not explain the complete revision
   effect or establish that the whole cost is paid only once per run.

### Inclusive constants timers and evidence limits

| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1000 | 0.8263 | 0.5833 | 2.6160 |

B-on includes digest (~1.25–1.26 s), publish (~1.31 s), subscribe (~2.56–2.57 s)
and settle (~1.28–1.29 s) timers. They are nested, inclusive and independently
maximized across ranks: do not sum them or call the whole difference IPC-map
latency. They implicate constants preparation/sharing in startup, without a
complete CUDA critical-path attribution.

B-off refresh by selecting the shared timer. B-off actually recorded
`RequestedConstants.refresh`; the table above uses that private timer. Original
artifacts are retained unchanged, with this correction explicit in the compact
evidence. The compact evidence also omits the upstream `within_5pct` flag for
absolute-time statistics; a percent-equivalence test applies to paired percent
changes, not seconds.
